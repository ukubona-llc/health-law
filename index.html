03
[Eco-System] Employer
PARTIAL DUTY

Legally Required (Employment Act 2006 + 2023 Amendments)

  • 100% salary: first month of illness
  • Job protection: up to 6 months (post-2023 extension from 2 months)
  • Medical certificate costs + transport: employer pays
  • No retaliatory dismissal for illness
  • NSSF contributions: 10% employer, 5% employee

NOT Legally Required

  • Paying for the biopsy itself
  • Medical insurance (unless contracted)
  • Coverage for dependants
  • Contribution to NHIS (private sector often exempt)
  • Funding non-occupational diagnostics

When Employer IS Liable for Procedure

  • Work-related illness / occupational exposure → Workers' Compensation Act
  • Medical cover explicitly in employment contract
  • Company policy / HR benefit scheme
  • Court-enforced duty of care (case-by-case)
The employer absorbs time-risk (sick leave, job continuity) but NOT health-risk (procedure costs). The structural logic: employer = labour market actor, not insurer. But for an employee with Kin=0, the employer is the only institution with daily contact — and therefore bears the greatest moral weight.

MGLSD / Labour Officers

NSSF Role

  • Retirement savings (accumulated)

  • Mid-term access: limited eligibility conditions
  • NOT a medical insurance fund

Ministry of Health / Uganda Cancer Institute

  • UCI provides subsidised/free diagnostics via referral
  • Core needle aspirate: public-sector priority procedure
  • Requires proper referral chain (often slow)

Industry / Labour Union Gap

  • Uganda's union coverage: low in private sector
  • Industry CBAs rarely mandate medical cover
  • No sector-wide health fund for most industries
  • OSH 2025: targets occupational disease, not general diagnostics
The regulator layer enforces employment rights but does not re-expand the financial scalar back into pooled health risk. It buffers job loss — not health cost. The coordination gap between Ministry of Labour and Ministry of Health is the institutional fracture line she falls through.

Uganda Health–Work Theorem (2026)
Uganda's system is legally strong at labour protection and structurally weak at health-risk pooling. When the Kin-layer collapses to zero — as it does for an orphaned urban employee — every biological event becomes a direct wage shock. The employer is the last standing institution, yet law places no obligation on them to absorb the medical cost itself. She is legally protected in her job; she is financially exposed in her body.

What She Can Actually Do — Right Now
01

Invoke Sick Leave Rights

Demand 100% salary for first month. Employer must pay — this is non-negotiable under Employment Act 2006.

02

Request UCI Public Referral

Uganda Cancer Institute provides subsidised core needle aspirates via public referral. District hospital referral letter required.

03

Ask Employer Directly

No legal obligation — but many employers will assist as goodwill. Frame as retention: replacing her costs more than her biopsy.

04

Check Employment Contract

Medical benefit may exist contractually even if she doesn't know. HR must disclose all contractual benefits on request.

05

Contact MGLSD Labour Officer

If employer obstructs sick leave or threatens job — a Labour Officer can intervene. Free service, district-based.

06

NGO / Hospital Social Work

Mulago and UCI both have social work departments for financial hardship cases. MUJHU, TASO, and others provide cancer diagnostic support.
